# Voice delivery: provider behavior and platform decisions

Reviewed against official documentation October 13, 2026 (Fish Drama3); Inworld review September 23, 2026. This file supersedes dated explanatory comments when they describe an older provider behavior. The implementation and request receipts remain the evidence for what actually runs.

## Inworld

The platform requests `inworld-tts-2`, which supports natural-language delivery instructions. Its `temperature` field is ignored; `deliveryMode` is the control for variability. Lively maps to `CREATIVE`, Balanced to `BALANCED`, and Steady to `STABLE`. Lively is our default after Kade preferred its engagement over the Balanced trial. A saved client choice wins. Speed remains a separate setting. Flash does not support delivery steering, so changing to Flash would lose a capability this platform needs.

A direction lasts until replaced or reset; punctuation, paragraph breaks, and sound events do not end it. We lift the opening direction into `instruction`, retain actual mid-text changes inline, and repeat the current instruction only when we create a separate provider request. Inworld prefers one mechanism per request; mixing the field and inline tags is nevertheless explicitly defined. Our mixed use preserves existing authored changes without extra splits or rewriting speech. Do not add a direction to every sentence.

`synthesisContext.previousRequests` supplies previous synthesized **text**, in order. It is not billed, and has a 2,000-character total limit. We keep at most three recent pieces within 1,900 characters for 90 seconds. Cross-request context is scoped to seat and resolved voice, previews never read or write it, and a request takes a fixed snapshot. Completion order cannot reverse the history. Failed requests are not remembered. Overlapping prefetches may lack a still-in-flight predecessor; this cache does not supply user audio or a full conversation. Old clients do not send a conversation ID, so the same voice on one seat may still share recent context across chats within the expiry window.

The clone/reference strongly affects habitual delivery. Lively permits variation; it does not by itself understand why a line should sound relieved, skeptical, or affectionate. Authored directions should describe the speaker's attitude toward the actual words. Emotional warmth does not require whispering, and excitement does not require shouting. Keep explicit performance requests possible. Cloning guidance differs between instant and professional clones; do not replace a person's chosen voice as a tuning shortcut.

Re-read October 17, 2026. The API reference for `/tts/v1/voice` no longer lists a request-level `instruction` field (it says to prefix the text with a bracket direction; only the OpenAI-compatible `/v1/audio/speech` documents an `instructions` field). The field still works: on October 17 the same sentence with `instruction: "say extremely slowly ..."` ran 15 s against 5 s plain, the same as the inline tag, and was billed 89 characters against 166 inline; an inline tag later in the text still replaces it. Keep the lift, and re-check with a duration test if Inworld changes the endpoint. Emphasis is capital letters on TTS-2 ("use sparingly"; double asterisks are read aloud). SSML `<break time="..."/>` is supported (at most 20 per request, 10 s each, and since Aug 6 it no longer ends a direction); there is no `[pause]` tag on Inworld, and a bracket that is not on the sound list becomes a lasting instruction.

The Inworld voice writer (`inworld-writer.js`, Oct 17 2026, Kade asked for it after the Fish director): an Inworld chat piece of at least `INWORLD_WRITER_MIN_CHARS` (140) characters is sent to `INWORLD_WRITER_MODEL` (MiMo 2.6 Flash on OpenRouter, Io Net first, then Makora and DeepInfra, zdr and data collection denied, thinking off) for Inworld's own companion style from its TTS-2 prompting guide: one fuller opening direction (sent in the `instruction` field, not billed), the stressed word in capitals, and at most one new bracket direction where the feeling changes. The direction always opens with Kade's words, "an animated casual chat with your best friend", plus the everyday feeling the words carry, in proportion to the moment (Kade, on her yes: "an animated or expressive casual conversation between best friends. Not a drama script being read by a voice actor."). Measured in Harley's voice: Inworld's layered "say gently ... warm and low with quiet comfort" stretched sentence stops to 1.0-1.2 s (0.6-0.8 s plain) and an excited "say with ..." slowed the words about 10%; over three takes each, "an animated casual chat with your best friend ..." ran as long as plain, "talking on the phone with a close friend ..." 1% longer and "chatting on the phone ..." 8% longer. Slowing and hushing words (calm, gentle, soft, low, measured, quiet and the like) and big dramatic words (bursting, flooded, overwhelmed, thrilled, hugely and the like) are taken out of a direction, and a mid-line direction that uses one is dropped. The model returns only snippets saying where markup goes, never the text, so no word can change; a snippet that does not match exactly once is skipped, and the result is kept only when the character's own tags stay where they were and the counts hold (at most two capitals, one per two sentences, one mid-line direction, none at the start). Short common words stay lower case (`normalizeInworldCaps` would undo them). Speed, pause, whisper/shout and sound words are taken out of the brief or drop it. No `<break>`: Inworld already stops 0.8-0.9 s between sentences in Harley's voice and a 400 ms break made it 1.1 s. A character's own opening direction wins over the brief. Sounds are still placed by `talkSoundsByParagraph`, seeded from the plain words so the markup never moves a sound. A failure, a host refusal, a timeout (`INWORLD_WRITER_TIMEOUT_MS`, 3 s) or doubt speaks the piece plain; 401/402/403/429 rest it 10 minutes. Results are cached by text. Chat speech and conversation auditions only: never reading, STABLE, book scenes, previews or the phone. `INWORLD_WRITER=1` (with `OPENROUTER_KEY`) turns it on; `/health` shows `inworldWriter`.

Casual Inworld delivery (Nov 5 2026; `inworld-pauses.js`, `inworld-steer.js`, `pace.js`; Kade: the Inworld voices "keep pausing and putting emphasis on weird words and slowing down and speeding up ... not like they're stopping to read stuff on paper every so often"). Findings: (1) Inworld's own docs make BALANCED the default and call CREATIVE "useful for ... barks or demo clips"; on the same words with no direction CREATIVE ran 6% slower, with median sentence stops of 240 ms against 160 and 2.0 stops of 600 ms or more per 100 words against 1.0. (2) Any instruction slows Inworld and lengthens its stops, and the character's one-word direction (three messages in four) costs 12-14% speed with no measurable gain in pitch or loudness spread; the old lively baseline wording was the slowest brief (-14%), and a brief that mentions stops ("only short natural stops") made the longest stops. (3) How much a word costs varies widely (clip length against no direction: bright .97, upbeat 1.00, amused 1.07, pleased 1.08, curious 1.11, calm 1.13, thoughtful 1.25, nostalgic 1.31, uneasy 1.50, uncertain 1.56, hesitant 1.61, sleepy 1.69), so a hesitant paragraph beside an encouraging one runs half again as long: that is the speeding up and slowing down. (4) The writer's capital letters add about 17% to a word that is already phrase-final and long (and in 8 of 14 cases the capitalised word was not among the six words with the biggest pitch, loudness and length peaks in its clip, so other words took the accent), and its mid-line direction, one message in five, re-steers the delivery partway. (5) An automatic `[breathe]` opened one message in four. (6) Inside a clip, quiet runs had a median of 495 ms and a p90 of 870 ms (longest 1,390 ms) on CREATIVE with a direction, against 330 and 593 on BALANCED with none.

What changed, each behind its own switch (all off or unchanged unless set): `TTS_DELIVERY_MODE=CREATIVE` (Lively) is the live default again: BALANCED was set on Nov 5 and put back on Nov 6 when Kade's ear said it was too flat (her phone sends `delivery=CREATIVE` anyway; Rollback: unset or `0` the switch you want back; `INWORLD_WRITER=1` restores the old writer, and `TTS_DELIVERY_MODE=BALANCED` is the calmer, flatter delivery that was tried first (the old delivery before this work was CREATIVE with the writer on).

A stray steering delimiter no longer turns the next tag into speech (`steering-balance.js`, Nov 6 2026; Kade, about the last message Kiana sent her: the voice said "warm and curious" aloud). The message held `...Two different problems.\n\n%%%\n%%%warm and curious%%% So what's the shape of it?`; `applySteeringTags` paired `%%%` runs left to right, so the stray one paired with the real tag's opener (what sat between them was a newline, which converts to nothing), the real closer was left over, the sweep removed the delimiters and the tag's words stayed in the speech, with the previous direction stamped in front (`[to correct with respect] warm and curious So what's...` in the live log). It affected Inworld and Fish alike and every later tag in the message flipped the same way. `balanceSteeringRuns()` now runs first and decides which runs are tags before anything pairs by position. The first version judged every pair by its words, and red teams proved it broke layouts the old pairing handled (`%%%excited!%%%`, `%%%\nwarm%%%`, empty `%%%%%%` tags, a fence), so the rules go in this order of weight: (1) a pair whose delimiters hug its words (`%%%excited!%%%`) is a tag whatever the words are, up to 400 characters and 70 words, because the old pairing always accepted that shape and a pair rejected on its words frees its closer to pair with the NEXT opener, which is the original bug again; (2) where a space or line break sits next to a delimiter the stray-or-tag question is real, so the words must look like a direction: at most 24 words and 200 characters, one line or a fence of up to three, not several sentences (an abbreviation's full stop ends no sentence in a lowercase span and does in a capitalised one); a capitalised sentence, question or exclamation of three words or more beside a run is speech, and so is a capitalised line beside a run when the reply writes tight tags, when the line is a sentence, a question or three words, or when only one side of it is a line break (a fence has a break on both); (3) polarity: a run with a space before it and a word right after it is shaped like an opener and cannot close the loose span in front of it when it really opens a tag of its own; (4) a speech-looking span gives way to a believable direction that follows it and, when the reply already writes tight tags, to the reply's own style if it has four words or more; a run of exactly two percent signs pairs only the way the old typo normaliser did (one line, 160 characters at most, the opener hugging a first word that starts with a letter or digit), so `half%% of it, then %% some`, `Yes%%%. Yeah,%%` and two doubled strays far apart stay prose; (5) a run of six or more percent signs that no word touches on its right is an empty tag and is dropped whole, and so are two runs with nothing but blanks, line breaks or marks between them when a space and anything but a lowercase letter follow the second (the iPhone re-stamps its carried bare newline as `%%%\n%%% ` in front of the next pieces; a lowercase start stays a padded direction), and so is a pair of three-sign runs hugging a short body with no letter in it (`%%%...%%%`); after a tag that hugs its words, a run of seven or more that no word touches is just a long closer (so is a run of six unless a lowercase direction follows it, because a direction starts with a lowercase letter and speech with a capital), otherwise it closes with its first three and the rest opens the next tag; (6) a tag whose closer was forgotten is closed for it, at the start of a sentence with speech right after one to four lowercase words (`%%%warm Hello there.`), or alone on a short line of plain lowercase words (ten at most, the longest real direction, the head of a tag a chunker cut off included), never when the words start like speech (yeah, okay, you, hola) and never for a line with a capital, a dash, a bracket or an emoji in it, which means the speech runs on (a line start counts as a sentence start unless the line before ended in a letter or comma without a blank line between); (7) the tail of a tag cut in half by a chunker (`curious%%% So what is the shape of it?`: a closer with no opener at the very start, after up to twelve lowercase words, followed by a capital or a line break) becomes a tag again, and a lone `%` at an end of a piece (the rest of a delimiter cut after one or two signs) is dropped: in a text that holds a `%%` run unless a number, in digits, fractions or words, stands before it (or alone on a line); in a text without one only where a cut delimiter is the likelier reading, before a blank and a capital at the start and after a sentence end or a line break at the end; (8) a dropped stray between two words leaves a space (not before punctuation, and not inside a word that has an apostrophe or hyphen in it, inside a number, or in front of the tail of a contraction such as n't). Requests over 150,000 characters are handed on untouched, and `KADE_STEERING_BALANCE=0` (a Railway variable and a redeploy) switches the whole balancer off, which is exactly the old pairing. When the balancer repairs a message the proxy logs one line, `[TTS] steering-balance: dropped N stray run(s), closed M unclosed tag(s)`, so "is it firing in real traffic" is one grep. `applySteeringTags` now hands every text with a `%` in it to the balancer (it returns a text without a `%%` run unchanged except for that lone `%` at an end), so the leftover `%` of a delimiter that a chunker cut in half no longer reaches the voice (over every cut position inside every real tag, no piece keeps a `%`, where the old pairing left one in 28,592), and the number-to-"percent" rule in `normalizeForSpeech` no longer eats the first percent sign of a delimiter that follows a number (`In 2019 %%%wistful%%%` used to say "2019 percent" and lose the tag). Known limits are listed at the top of `steering-balance.js`: most are the old pairing's too, and none shows up in the real traffic, whose 7,148 tags are all tight, lowercase and at a paragraph start; three strays in one reply, strays of six or more percent signs glued to words, and capitalised or fenced directions (a fence closed by a doubled `%%` on its own line included, which only the old one-line typo rule could pair) can still be read as speech, a tag cut in half is repaired only when its tail starts the text, and the iPhone's re-stamp is not repaired when the phone built it from speech. Tested: the incident and the shapes red teams found (about 100) through the real handler on both Inworld lanes and Fish; seeded fuzz in which no direction word is ever left in the speech; a differential test that runs the shipped `applySteeringTags` with and without the balancer on production-shaped replies (identical on a clean reply; with one stray run of any size from 2 to 6 at any natural position, or two strays of sizes 2 to 5 with one glued to a tag closer, never worse than the old pairing: 0 worse and about 4,400 and 7,100 repaired in every 15,000 injections); the real messages (all 6,607 real audition scripts hand the voice byte-identical requests on the old and the fixed code, and the same messages damaged on purpose in 20 ways keep their speech: the incident shape 100% against 0% for the old pairing, a tag cut in half 100% against 0%, a tag on its own lines 100% against 0% for the first version); and a hand mutation sweep of the rules (every mutant that is not equivalent fails a test).

Proxy robustness fixes (Nov 6 2026, the odds and ends four red-team rounds found while the steering-tag leak was fixed; one commit each, `git revert <sha>` undoes exactly that one): (1) a speech request whose `input` is not a string answers 400 `Invalid field: input must be a string` instead of ending the Node process (`stripThinkingBlock` ran outside any try/catch in an async Express 4 handler, `6aeaa8e`); (2) `normalizeForSpeech`'s email rule (`speakEmails`, anchored on the `@`) and `stripCitationMarkers`' blank-before-punctuation rule are linear, and the second no longer deletes line breaks (a paragraph that opened with `...` or `:)` used to be glued to the one above it, `1f4ca74`); (3) `rescueLooseDirections` no longer turns a `(laughs)` or `*sighs*` inside a `%%%` tag into a tag nested in it (`%%%dry (softly) and flat%%%` is the one direction "dry softly and flat"; a clock or a silent gesture still goes, and `%%%warm (laughs)%%%` is now "warm laughs" instead of a direction plus a laugh sound), and `sanitizeDirectionText` drops `[` and `]` (`30dca6e`); (4) `splitSentences` loses no word (`Version 3.5 is out.` came back as `5 is out.`) and ends no sentence on a stop inside a short `[direction]`, and `groupSentences` cuts in front of a direction, never inside it (`2885bd9`); (5) a stray `%%%` alone on its line between two lines of text takes its line with it, so the carry-forward cap counts the paragraphs the reply has (`e936b4d`). Tests: `input-type-wire`, `prep-linear` (clock tests at 149,000 characters), `rescue-nesting`, `sentence-brackets`, `stray-line`; each rule was mutation-swept. Order in `fishSynthesizeChunk`: the run-on guard judges the take unstretched with its pauses capped, the length its limits were measured on; the Fish slot is given back; Praat then stretches the take from before the pause cap and the cap runs again on the result, so a capped silence is the pace's cap and not the cap sped up. One long-lived worker, started at boot from `FISH_STRETCH_PYTHON` (`/opt/fish-stretch/bin/python` in the image; praat-parselmouth 0.4.7, numpy 2.4.6), works one clip at a time; `FISH_STRETCH_TIMEOUT_MS` (default 2000) is the longest any clip waits. If the worker is missing, dead, slow or wrong the clip goes out unstretched, exactly as it would have with the switch off, one short log line with no words says why, and the worker is started again after 1 s, doubling to 30 s; the wait starts over only once a worker has answered 5 clips and stayed up a minute. A failing worker is silent to the listener, so `/health` carries `fishStretch` (`on`, `ready`, `asked`, `stretched`, `kept`, `restarts`, `lastProblem`): read it after a deploy. Added delay measured on Kade's PC: about 20 ms for a 6 s clip, 72 ms for 25 s. Praat randomizes unvoiced sound, so two stretches of one take are the same length but not byte-identical.

The Fish run-on guard (`fish-runaway.js`, Oct 18 2026): Drama3 on a cloned reference sometimes runs on after a short line, repeating it or reading on into the reference recording's own words (Kade's voice: "Audio description by Kade-AI." ran on in 4 of 16 takes, then 2 of 4, up to the ~47.6 s ceiling). Every Fish take is measured and logged (`[TTS] fish chunk length:`): the characters Fish was asked to say (the sent text without `[directions]` and `<tags>`), sound and pause tags, seconds, voiced seconds, pace, voice and limit. A take longer than 3 s + 0.16 s per spoken character + 1.5 s per sound or pause tag ran on: it is asked for once more with a fresh request (this does not use up the provider-error retries), and if that one runs on too, the chunk fails with "Fish ran on twice for this chunk ..." instead of being spoken. The limit comes from logged data: 1,036 normal clips (582 voices, Unhurried to Quick, little sounds on) never crossed it, the slowest with 1.39x headroom, and all 13 measured run-ons did. A line said only twice can still pass, and from about 280 characters the limit is past the ceiling. `FISH_RUNAWAY_GUARD=retry` (default), `log` (measure and warn, keep the take) or `off` (no measuring); `/health` shows `fishRunaway`, and `chunksToday` counts `ranOn` takes and `ranOnFixed` chunks.

Conversation versus reading (October 16, 2026). Chat speech, meaning anything that is not a voice preview, a lab preset, a STABLE request, or a Library `reading:` session, can get two extras. First, Fish gets a voice-actor brief from `FISH_DEFAULT_DIRECTION`, which is live (October 17, her pick from four tested wordings) as "telling a close friend all about it on the phone, letting every feeling show in your voice". The brief is skipped when a request opens with the character's own direction. Second, spoken filler words, controlled by `FISH_TALK_FILLERS=1` (live) and `INWORLD_TALK_FILLERS=1` (off). Each paragraph gets at most one filler, and never in its first sentence, in lists, in quoted passages, or inside tags. The fillers change only the words sent to the provider, never the reply people read. A request's own `fillers` true/false, or the `x-kade-tts-fillers` header, overrides the server default; this is the listener's switch. Blind paired judging (gemini-4-argon, which correctly ranked a narrator decoy lower, 4 to 14) scored the brief at 18 to 7 against no brief, and the brief plus fillers at 26 to 3. Dropping the added sentence pauses, a lower temperature, and Inworld's adjective-style baseline did not help. `/health` reports `fishTalk` and `inworldTalk`. Fish temperature is 1.0 with top-p 1.0 for chat since October 17 (160 clips at four settings showed no errors). STABLE still maps to 0.5, and `FISH_TEMPERATURE_MAX` is a ceiling over every delivery.

Later on October 16 two more pieces were added. The first is little human sounds: `FISH_TALK_SOUNDS` / `INWORLD_TALK_SOUNDS`, plus a request's own `sounds` true or false. A request with `fillers` false turns them off as well. They follow these rules:
- At most one sound per paragraph, placed by the words: a small laugh right after a plainly funny line, a sigh before a tired or relieved line (never in front of "phew" or "ugh"), a breath at an opening or before a long sentence, and a throat clear rarely.
- Each engine gets its own spelling. Inworld only takes words from its sound list (`[breathe]`, `[chuckle]`, `[sigh]`, `[clear throat]`). Drama3's spellings can be changed with `FISH_SOUND_*`.
- In paired judging, `[small laugh]` after a funny line won 5 to 1. `[clear throat]` came out as a cough and lost 2 to 4, and `[quietly clears throat]` won 5 to 1.

The second is auditions. A conversation audition now counts as talk and gets the same treatment as a voice message from that voice: speed, delivery, fillers and sounds. Book scenes stay plain (`KIND_BY_SCRIPT` in audition-scenes.js).

## Verification and limits

Regression cases must include a mid-piece change, inline reset, one-shot sound, long paragraph, different seat/voice, preview, overlapping calls, and an explicit delivery override. The iPhone and live-call splitters also track the final authored direction, not only the opening one. Their carry limits remain intact. Android already scans all authored tags when carrying between chunks.

Audio trials use identical scripts on both providers at Balanced and Lively.
