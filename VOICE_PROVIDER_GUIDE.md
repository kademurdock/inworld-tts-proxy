# Voice delivery: provider behavior and platform decisions

Reviewed against official documentation October 13, 2026 (Fish Drama3); Inworld review September 23, 2026. This file supersedes dated explanatory comments when they describe an older provider behavior. The implementation and request receipts remain the evidence for what actually runs.

## Inworld

The platform requests `inworld-tts-2`, which supports natural-language delivery instructions. Its `temperature` field is ignored; `deliveryMode` is the control for variability. Lively maps to `CREATIVE`, Balanced to `BALANCED`, and Steady to `STABLE`. Lively is our default after Kade preferred its engagement over the Balanced trial. A saved client choice wins. Speed remains a separate setting. Flash does not support delivery steering, so changing to Flash would lose a capability this platform needs.

A direction lasts until replaced or reset; punctuation, paragraph breaks, and sound events do not end it. We lift the opening direction into `instruction`, retain actual mid-text changes inline, and repeat the current instruction only when we create a separate provider request. Inworld prefers one mechanism per request; mixing the field and inline tags is nevertheless explicitly defined. Our mixed use preserves existing authored changes without extra splits or rewriting speech. Do not add a direction to every sentence.

`synthesisContext.previousRequests` supplies previous synthesized **text**, in order. It is not billed, and has a 2,000-character total limit. We keep at most three recent pieces within 1,900 characters for 90 seconds. Cross-request context is scoped to seat and resolved voice, previews never read or write it, and a request takes a fixed snapshot. Completion order cannot reverse the history. Failed requests are not remembered. Overlapping prefetches may lack a still-in-flight predecessor; this cache does not supply user audio or a full conversation. Old clients do not send a conversation ID, so the same voice on one seat may still share recent context across chats within the expiry window.

The clone/reference strongly affects habitual delivery. Lively permits variation; it does not by itself understand why a line should sound relieved, skeptical, or affectionate. Authored directions should describe the speaker's attitude toward the actual words. Emotional warmth does not require whispering, and excitement does not require shouting. Keep explicit performance requests possible. Cloning guidance differs between instant and professional clones; do not replace a person's chosen voice as a tuning shortcut.

Re-read October 17, 2026. The API reference for `/tts/v1/voice` no longer lists a request-level `instruction` field (it says to prefix the text with a bracket direction; only the OpenAI-compatible `/v1/audio/speech` documents an `instructions` field). The field still works: on October 17 the same sentence with `instruction: "say extremely slowly ..."` ran 15 s against 5 s plain, the same as the inline tag, and was billed 89 characters against 166 inline; an inline tag later in the text still replaces it. Keep the lift, and re-check with a duration test if Inworld changes the endpoint. Emphasis is capital letters on TTS-2 ("use sparingly"; double asterisks are read aloud). SSML `<break time="..."/>` is supported (at most 20 per request, 10 s each, and since Aug 6 it no longer ends a direction); there is no `[pause]` tag on Inworld, and a bracket that is not on the sound list becomes a lasting instruction.

The Inworld voice writer (`inworld-writer.js`, Oct 17 2026, Kade asked for it after the Fish director): an Inworld chat piece of at least `INWORLD_WRITER_MIN_CHARS` (140) characters is sent to `INWORLD_WRITER_MODEL` (MiMo 2.6 Flash on OpenRouter, Io Net first, then Makora and DeepInfra, zdr and data collection denied, thinking off) for Inworld's own companion style from its TTS-2 prompting guide: one fuller opening direction (sent in the `instruction` field, not billed), the stressed word in capitals, and at most one new bracket direction where the feeling changes. The direction always opens with Kade's words, "an animated casual chat with your best friend", plus the everyday feeling the words carry, in proportion to the moment (Kade, on her yes: "an animated or expressive casual conversation between best friends. Slowing and hushing words (calm, gentle, soft, low, measured, quiet and the like) and big dramatic words (bursting, flooded, overwhelmed, thrilled, hugely and the like) are taken out of a direction, and a mid-line direction that uses one is dropped. The model returns only snippets saying where markup goes, never the text, so no word can change; a snippet that does not match exactly once is skipped, and the result is kept only when the character's own tags stay where they were and the counts hold (at most two capitals, one per two sentences, one mid-line direction, none at the start). Short common words stay lower case (`normalizeInworldCaps` would undo them). Speed, pause, whisper/shout and sound words are taken out of the brief or drop it. No `<break>`: Inworld already stops 0.8-0.9 s between sentences in Harley's voice and a 400 ms break made it 1.1 s. A character's own opening direction wins over the brief. Sounds are still placed by `talkSoundsByParagraph`, seeded from the plain words so the markup never moves a sound. Chat speech and conversation auditions only: never reading, STABLE, book scenes, previews or the phone. `INWORLD_WRITER=1` (with `OPENROUTER_KEY`) turns it on; `/health` shows `inworldWriter`.

A stray steering delimiter no longer turns the next tag into speech (`steering-balance.js`, Nov 6 2026; Kade, about the last message Kiana sent her: the voice said "warm and curious" aloud). The message held `...Two different problems.\n\n%%%\n%%%warm and curious%%% So what's the shape of it?`; `applySteeringTags` paired `%%%` runs left to right, so the stray one paired with the real tag's opener (what sat between them was a newline, which converts to nothing), the real closer was left over, the sweep removed the delimiters and the tag's words stayed in the speech, with the previous direction stamped in front (`[to correct with respect] warm and curious So what's...` in the live log). It affected Inworld and Fish alike and every later tag in the message flipped the same way. `balanceSteeringRuns()` now runs first and decides which runs are tags before anything pairs by position. The first version judged every pair by its words, and red teams proved it broke layouts the old pairing handled (`%%%excited!%%%`, `%%%\nwarm%%%`, empty `%%%%%%` tags, a fence), so the rules go in this order of weight: (1) a pair whose delimiters hug its words (`%%%excited!%%%`) is a tag whatever the words are, up to 400 characters and 70 words, because the old pairing always accepted that shape and a pair rejected on its words frees its closer to pair with the NEXT opener, which is the original bug again; (2) where a space or line break sits next to a delimiter the stray-or-tag question is real, so the words must look like a direction: at most 24 words and 200 characters, one line or a fence of up to three, not several sentences (an abbreviation's full stop ends no sentence in a lowercase span and does in a capitalised one); a capitalised sentence, question or exclamation of three words or more beside a run is speech, and so is a capitalised line beside a run when the reply writes tight tags, when the line is a sentence, a question or three words, or when only one side of it is a line break (a fence has a break on both); (3) polarity: a run with a space before it and a word right after it is shaped like an opener and cannot close the loose span in front of it when it really opens a tag of its own; (4) a speech-looking span gives way to a believable direction that follows it and, when the reply already writes tight tags, to the reply's own style if it has four words or more; a run of exactly two percent signs pairs only the way the old typo normaliser did (one line, 160 characters at most, the opener hugging a first word that starts with a letter or digit), so `half%% of it, then %% some`, `Yes%%%. Yeah,%%` and two doubled strays far apart stay prose; (5) a run of six or more percent signs that no word touches on its right is an empty tag and is dropped whole, and so are two runs with nothing but blanks, line breaks or marks between them when a space and anything but a lowercase letter follow the second (the iPhone re-stamps its carried bare newline as `%%%\n%%% ` in front of the next pieces; a lowercase start stays a padded direction), and so is a pair of three-sign runs hugging a short body with no letter in it (`%%%...%%%`); after a tag that hugs its words, a run of seven or more that no word touches is just a long closer (so is a run of six unless a lowercase direction follows it, because a direction starts with a lowercase letter and speech with a capital), otherwise it closes with its first three and the rest opens the next tag; (6) a tag whose closer was forgotten is closed for it, at the start of a sentence with speech right after one to four lowercase words (`%%%warm Hello there.`), or alone on a short line of plain lowercase words (ten at most, the longest real direction, the head of a tag a chunker cut off included), never when the words start like speech (yeah, okay, you, hola) and never for a line with a capital, a dash, a bracket or an emoji in it, which means the speech runs on (a line start counts as a sentence start unless the line before ended in a letter or comma without a blank line between); (7) the tail of a tag cut in half by a chunker (`curious%%% So what is the shape of it?`: a closer with no opener at the very start, after up to twelve lowercase words, followed by a capital or a line break) becomes a tag again, and a lone `%` at an end of a piece (the rest of a delimiter cut after one or two signs) is dropped: in a text that holds a `%%` run unless a number, in digits, fractions or words, stands before it (or alone on a line); in a text without one only where a cut delimiter is the likelier reading, before a blank and a capital at the start and after a sentence end or a line break at the end; (8) a dropped stray between two words leaves a space (not before punctuation, and not inside a word that has an apostrophe or hyphen in it, inside a number, or in front of the tail of a contraction such as n't). Requests over 150,000 characters are handed on untouched, and `KADE_STEERING_BALANCE=0` (a Railway variable and a redeploy) switches the whole balancer off, which is exactly the old pairing. When the balancer repairs a message the proxy logs one line, `[TTS] steering-balance: dropped N stray run(s), closed M unclosed tag(s)`, so "is it firing in real traffic" is one grep. `applySteeringTags` now hands every text with a `%` in it to the balancer (it returns a text without a `%%` run unchanged except for that lone `%` at an end), so the leftover `%` of a delimiter that a chunker cut in half no longer reaches the voice (over every cut position inside every real tag, no piece keeps a `%`, where the old pairing left one in 28,592), and the number-to-"percent" rule in `normalizeForSpeech` no longer eats the first percent sign of a delimiter that follows a number (`In 2019 %%%wistful%%%` used to say "2019 percent" and lose the tag). Route guard (Nov 7 2026; an independent review of the proxy found 25 malformed-request shapes on 8 routes that END THE PROCESS, all behind a secret, and about 90 async handlers with no guard of their own; commits, newest first to revert: `7826a46` the allow-list for an error's headers and the net's starting-up rule, `322849b` the second review round's fixes, `a34c4fc` the final handler as the app's own `done`, `3326773` the last net, `3e69408` hardening after the review, `61ca078` tests, `6712e26` the transcription route, `730a450` the final error handler, `56c426a` the Layer patch; revert newest first: later commits touch lines of earlier ones, and all of them lean on the Layer patch): Express 4 never looks at what a route handler returns, so an async handler that throws (a body field of the wrong type is enough: `{"branch":5}`, `{"input":{"toString":1}}`, a lone surrogate in a key; a query string like `?limit[toString]=1` or one header of two-byte characters as long as the approvals secret did the same; that header needs no secret at all, the 25 shapes of the review are all behind one) is a rejected promise nobody listens to, and Node exits on it for every listener; Railway restarts a crashed service at most three times (`railway.toml`: `restartPolicyMaxRetries = 3`) and then stops. `async-routes.js` patches `Layer.prototype.handle_request` and `handle_error` of `express/lib/router/layer` once, at the top of `server.js` before any router is mounted (the express-async-errors technique), so the rejection of any handler goes to `next(err)` like a synchronous throw always did; a failure that is falsy or the string "route" or "router" becomes an Error, because Express reads those as "carry on to the next route". A rejection that comes after the answer is already out is handled on the spot by the same function that ends the others (the router may have moved on, and Express's own handler would destroy the socket). `installFinalHandler(app)`, called right after `server.js` makes the app, makes `finalErrorHandler` the `done` that `app.handle` gives the router, which is where Express puts its own final handler. It is NOT a layer at the end of the stack: such a layer is visited by every request no route answers and makes Express 4 defer that request's `done` by a `setImmediate`, so big JSON bodies piled up in memory (eight concurrent 10 MB bodies, each about 175 MB of heap once parsed, ended a 1 GB process; found by the review, kept out by `route-stack-wire.test.js` and a counted-`setImmediate` test). A request nothing answered still gets Express's own 404 (the same finalhandler, the same bytes) and OPTIONS keeps its automatic Allow answer. `finalErrorHandler` logs the cause (`[proxy] POST /path failed with 500: ...` and up to five real stack frames; method and path, never the query string; control, format and line-separator characters in the cause become spaces, and a frame line is cut at 300 characters; a thrown Symbol or an object without a prototype is fine), answers 500 `{"error":"Proxy internal error"}` with no stack and no message, keeps any 4xx on `err.status` or `err.statusCode` (body-parser's 400 `{"error":"Bad Request"}` and 413 `{"error":"Payload Too Large"}`, Express's 400 for a bad percent-escape, the 404 of a `sendFile` whose file is missing), turns a 5xx status on the error into 500, sends the error's own Content-Range, Retry-After, Allow and WWW-Authenticate headers with a 4xx (a 416's Content-Range tells a resuming downloader the size; nothing else an error carries goes out) and none with a 500, ignores a status the handler had already set, removes the headers the failing handler set that described its own answer (type, length, disposition, encoding, language, range, ETag, Last-Modified, Accept-Ranges, Cache-Control) and adds `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, and it answers at once instead of waiting for the rest of an unread request body, as Express's own handler did; all of these used to be an HTML page, with the stack when NODE_ENV is not production. When the answer is already out it only closes it: a finished one is left alone (the socket may carry other requests), a chunked one gets a clean short end, and one that announced a Content-Length and stopped short is destroyed so the client hears at once. POST /v1/audio/transcriptions: busboy destroys the open file stream when an upload stops before its closing boundary and nothing listened for 'error' on it (an uncaught event, so no rejection handler could help), and its 'close' event then answered 200 after the 400 (ERR_HTTP_HEADERS_SENT in an async callback); both now go through one `refuse()` and a `headersSent` check, and `refuse()` reads and drops the rest of the body (left alone, a refused upload of more than about 150 KB held its keep-alive connection until the 5 s timeout). `rejection-net.js` is the last layer, for a rejection that belongs to no request (a timer, a background job, code added later): one `unhandledRejection` listener that logs it (`[proc] unhandled rejection #N`, its cause and five frames) and lets the proxy go on, under three rules: the first 30 seconds are fatal (a deploy that cannot start must fail its health check), `/health` carries `unhandledRejections { total, recent, windowSeconds }` (the status is untouched), and more than 20 in a minute (counted on a monotonic clock) ends the process (exit 1). It does not answer a request: nothing in the proxy has a rejection that belongs to one (a read of all the non-route promise and event code found none a request can reach), and new async code that touches a response must catch its own failures. The first try at a net (branch `claude/proxy-residuals-first-try`) was rejected because, without the guards above, it turned a crash into a request that is never answered (19 hung 10 MB requests took the process from 84 MB to 567 MB) and let a failing boot look healthy. `PROXY_BOOT_GRACE_MS`, `PROXY_REJECTION_WINDOW_MS` and `PROXY_REJECTION_LIMIT` exist for the tests and are not set on Railway. Known and left (found by the review or the audit, none caused by this work): the global `express.json({ limit: "10mb" })` parses before any auth, so a 10 KB gzip body that inflates to a 10 MB JSON array stalls the event loop for seconds and many big bodies at once cost memory; `speech-gate.js` runs a quadratic regex on the first `X-Forwarded-For` element (a 16 KB header costs 200 to 330 ms of CPU) and keys its `refused` map by path, user agent, origin and caller without a cap before the ten-minute summary; dozens of answers echo `err.message` (more than eighty call sites in `librechat.js` alone; the speech route's own catch adds it as `details`), so the "no message from the cause" promise above is for the answers this work writes; `/help*` answers 500 when the Host header is missing; `httpsReq` in `librechat.js` (avatar-gen) waits forever when the connection resets in the middle of a body (a hang, not a crash); the transcription route caps one file at 160 MB but not the number of file parts or the total (every part is held in memory) and its Deepgram `fetch` has no timeout; `createLimiter`'s `.finally` chain in `server.js` has no `.catch` (unreachable today, every caller passes an async function); `app.listen` has no 'error' listener (a bad port should stop the boot anyway); the transcription route reads `res.headersSent` as "already refused" (on a destroyed response it stays false, and no way to make busboy 1.6.0 report an error after the socket closed was found).

The fillers change only the words sent to the provider, never the reply people read. A request's own `fillers` true/false, or the `x-kade-tts-fillers` header, overrides the server default; this is the listener's switch. Blind paired judging (gemini-4-argon, which correctly ranked a narrator decoy lower, 4 to 14) scored the brief at 18 to 7 against no brief, and the brief plus fillers at 26 to 3. Dropping the added sentence pauses, a lower temperature, and Inworld's adjective-style baseline did not help. `/health` reports `fishTalk` and `inworldTalk`. Fish temperature is 1.0 with top-p 1.0 for chat since October 17 (160 clips at four settings showed no errors). STABLE still maps to 0.5, and `FISH_TEMPERATURE_MAX` is a ceiling over every delivery.

Later on October 16 two more pieces were added. The first is little human sounds: `FISH_TALK_SOUNDS` / `INWORLD_TALK_SOUNDS`, plus a request's own `sounds` true or false. A request with `fillers` false turns them off as well. They follow these rules:
- At most one sound per paragraph, placed by the words: a small laugh right after a plainly funny line, a sigh before a tired or relieved line (never in front of "phew" or "ugh"), a breath at an opening or before a long sentence, and a throat clear rarely.
- Each engine gets its own spelling. Inworld only takes words from its sound list (`[breathe]`, `[chuckle]`, `[sigh]`, `[clear throat]`). Drama3's spellings can be changed with `FISH_SOUND_*`.
- In paired judging, `[small laugh]` after a funny line won 5 to 1. `[clear throat]` came out as a cough and lost 2 to 4, and `[quietly clears throat]` won 5 to 1.

The second is auditions. A conversation audition now counts as talk and gets the same treatment as a voice message from that voice: speed, delivery, fillers and sounds. Book scenes stay plain (`KIND_BY_SCRIPT` in audition-scenes.js).

The API documents `condition_on_previous_chunks` (default true) for audio continuity **inside a request**. It is not a previous-conversation-text field. Do not prepend earlier dialogue to Fish's `text`: it would be spoken again. The current separate HTTP requests cannot share that internal audio state. Longer requests or WebSocket contexts need fresh fidelity and latency trials before replacing the measured short-request path. No quality-guard flag or new paid voice training was enabled.

Published Drama3 API pricing is $10 per million characters through November 12, 2026, then $50 per million. This is separate from creator subscription credits. Previous paid S2.1 Pro pricing was $15 per million UTF-8 bytes. The proxy does not calculate user charges from a hard-coded Fish rate. These are published rates, not an invoice measurement. Self-hosting requires a separate hardware and license assessment; open weights do not remove the Community License's restriction on providing a TTS or cloning service to others.

Fish's API schema currently says normal latency and chunk length 300 are defaults, while its prose guide gives balanced and 200. We explicitly send normal latency and treat the API schema/request receipts as authoritative. Its model overview describes broader S2 language support than the older emotion reference. ## Verification and limits

Regression cases must include a mid-piece change, inline reset, one-shot sound, long paragraph, different seat/voice, preview, overlapping calls, and an explicit delivery override. The iPhone and live-call splitters also track the final authored direction, not only the opening one. Their carry limits remain intact. Android already scans all authored tags when carrying between chunks.

Audio trials use identical scripts on both providers at Balanced and Lively. Check words through transcription and inspect audio boundaries/levels; neither test proves that emotion sounds appropriate. Listening remains the acceptance check for warmth, identity, and naturalness. TestFlight compilation also does not prove phone playback. Existing cached/downloaded recordings retain their original performance; fresh synthesis uses the corrections.

Jev can classify text or review a proposed wording change. It does not hear this audio and must not be presented as an acoustic emotion judge.
